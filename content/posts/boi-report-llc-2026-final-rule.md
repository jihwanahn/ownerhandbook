**Short answer:** no, not if your company was created in the United States. FinCEN's **final rule, effective August 14, 2026**, permanently exempts every LLC, corporation, and similar entity formed under U.S. state or tribal law from beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting. That covers new companies, companies that already filed, and companies that never filed. You don't need to file, update, or correct a BOI report. If you got a FinCEN ID as a U.S. person, you don't need to keep it updated either. The only companies that still report are those **formed under a foreign country's law** that have registered to do business in a U.S. state, and even they don't report their U.S. owners.

This guide is for owners of small U.S. LLCs and corporations, including people forming one before year-end, who keep seeing "BOI deadline" warnings and want a straight answer. It covers the federal Corporate Transparency Act rule and one state law that copied it (New York). It doesn't cover the reporting duties of foreign companies in detail.

# What changed, in order

The rule went through several reversals, which is why so much old advice is still floating around. Here's the sequence from the Federal Register and FinCEN's own pages:

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| January 1, 2024 | FinCEN's original reporting rule took effect. Most U.S. LLCs and corporations would have had to report their owners. |
| Late 2024 to early 2025 | Federal courts paused the rule, then FinCEN extended most deadlines to March 21, 2025. |
| March 2, 2025 | Treasury announced it would not enforce the rule against U.S. citizens, domestic companies, or their owners. |
| March 26, 2025 | FinCEN's **interim final rule** removed domestic companies from the definition of "reporting company." |
| August 11, 2026 | FinCEN issued the **final rule** making that exemption permanent. |
| August 14, 2026 | The final rule was published in the Federal Register (91 FR 52508) and **took effect** the same day. |

So if you read in 2024 that every small LLC had to report by January 1, 2025, that advice is out of date. FinCEN's BOI page now says plainly that U.S. companies "are no longer required to file BOI reports."

# Who is exempt

FinCEN's BOI page says **all entities created in the United States**, including those once called "domestic reporting companies," **and their beneficial owners** are exempt. In practice that includes:

- a **single-member LLC** taxed as a sole proprietorship,
- a **multi-member LLC** taxed as a partnership,
- an LLC that elected **S corporation** status, and a regular corporation,
- a U.S. LLC whose owners are **not U.S. citizens**. The exemption turns on where the company was created, not on who owns it.

The final rule also added two things the March 2025 interim rule didn't have. U.S. persons who got a **FinCEN ID** don't have to update or correct the information they gave to get it. And foreign companies that still report don't have to list **U.S. person company applicants** (the people who filed their registration paperwork).

Forming your LLC in December? The exemption applies from day one, so BOI is off your formation checklist. Our guide on [starting an LLC in December](https://ownerhandbook.blogspot.com/2026/11/starting-llc-in-december-file-now-or.html) covers the date choices that still matter, and the [LLC cost breakdown](https://ownerhandbook.blogspot.com/2026/10/how-much-does-llc-really-cost-2026.html) covers the state fees you do still pay.

# Who still has to report

Under the final rule, a "reporting company" is only an entity that:

1. was **formed under the law of a foreign country**, and
2. has **registered to do business** in a U.S. state or tribal jurisdiction by filing a document with a secretary of state or similar office,

and that doesn't qualify for one of the existing exemptions.

Those companies have **30 calendar days** after their U.S. registration becomes effective to file an initial report. They report the company's own details, including its legal name, any trade names, its address, its foreign jurisdiction of formation, and its tax ID. They report beneficial owners **only if those owners aren't U.S. persons**. FinCEN's final-rule Q&A says a beneficial owner is an individual who owns or controls at least 25% of the company, or exercises substantial control over it.

If that describes your company, read FinCEN's final-rule Q&A and its BOI page before deciding. This guide doesn't walk through the foreign-company filing.

# If you already filed a BOI report

The final rule says FinCEN received about 15 million reports from domestic companies before the interim rule took effect. It also says what happens to that data:

- **You don't need to update or correct it.** Domestic companies aren't reporting companies anymore, so the update duty is gone.
- **FinCEN plans to delete it.** Treasury said FinCEN will delete information about individuals, whether beneficial owners, company applicants, or FinCEN ID holders, that it reasonably believes is a U.S. person's, for example where a U.S. passport or driver's license was used.
- **You don't have to ask.** The final rule says FinCEN doesn't plan to require or request deletion requests, and it won't send individual confirmations. It will post a notice on its website when the deletion is done.
- **Don't file now "just in case."** FinCEN plans to run the deletion once. The final rule says that if U.S. company or U.S. person information is filed after **February 10, 2027**, FinCEN doesn't expect to delete it.

# Your bank may still ask about owners

This catches people off guard. The BOI exemption covers reports **to FinCEN**. It doesn't change the separate **Customer Due Diligence (CDD) Rule**, which requires covered financial institutions to collect beneficial ownership information from their business customers when they open an account. FinCEN's final-rule Q&A calls that continuing bank requirement an important part of the system.

So when you open a business checking account, expect the bank to ask who owns and controls the company. That's the bank's own rule, not a FinCEN filing, and it's not a sign that you missed something. Have your formation documents and your [EIN](https://ownerhandbook.blogspot.com/2026/10/what-is-ein-number-and-how-do-i-get-one.html) ready. If you trade under a brand name, the bank may also ask for your [DBA filing](https://ownerhandbook.blogspot.com/2026/11/do-i-need-dba-assumed-name-and.html).

# Letters and emails about BOI: the red flags

The rule changes have made BOI a favorite topic for scammers. FinCEN's BOI page lists specific warning signs:

![FinCEN's red flags for BOI scam letters: any form numbered 4022 or 5102, which FinCEN does not have; a sender called the US Business Regulations Dept., which is not a government agency; a request for a fee, although filing directly with FinCEN is free; a link or QR code to pay or file; a first notice about penalties by email or phone](assets/images/boi-scam-red-flags.webp "Warning signs FinCEN lists for BOI-related fraud. Source: FinCEN.gov, Beneficial Ownership Information Reporting, fraud alert.")

- **"Form 4022" or "Form 5102."** FinCEN says it has no forms with those numbers.
- **"US Business Regulations Dept."** FinCEN says there's no government entity by that name.
- **A fee.** FinCEN says there is no fee to file BOI directly with FinCEN, and it doesn't send letters asking for payment to file.
- **A link or QR code.** FinCEN warns against clicking suspicious links or scanning QR codes in BOI letters and emails.
- **A penalty notice by email or phone.** FinCEN says it doesn't send initial correspondence about penalties that way.

Now that U.S. companies don't file at all, any message telling a U.S. LLC that it must file a BOI report, pay a BOI fee, or avoid a BOI penalty deserves suspicion. Don't reply with personal information. Look up the agency's contact details yourself instead of using the ones in the message.

# Don't forget New York

New York passed its own **LLC Transparency Act** (Limited Liability Company Law sections 1106 to 1108), which took effect **January 1, 2026**. According to the New York Department of State, the disclosure requirement applies to non-exempt LLCs **formed under the law of a foreign country** and authorized to do business in New York. The filing fee is **$25**.

Like the federal rule, it reaches only LLCs formed outside the United States, so an LLC formed in New York or another U.S. state isn't covered. If your LLC was formed outside the United States and does business in New York, check the Department of State's beneficial owner disclosure page for the filing deadlines and forms. Other states could pass their own rules later, so watch for notices from your secretary of state, the office you already deal with for [state business registrations](https://ownerhandbook.blogspot.com/2026/10/do-i-need-business-license-federal.html).

# Quick checklist

- Confirm where your company was **formed**. If it's a U.S. state, you don't file a federal BOI report.
- If you already filed, **do nothing**. No updates, no corrections, no deletion request.
- **Don't file a new report** for a U.S. company "to be safe."
- Treat any BOI letter that asks for money, uses Form 4022 or 5102, or threatens penalties as a likely scam.
- Expect your **bank** to ask about owners when you open a business account. That's normal.
- If your company was formed **outside the United States** and registered in a state, read FinCEN's Q&A and, for New York, the Department of State's page.
