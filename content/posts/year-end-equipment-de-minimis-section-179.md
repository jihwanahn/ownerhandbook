**Short answer:** for a small business without audited financial statements, an item costing **$2,500 or less per invoice or per item** can usually be deducted right away under the IRS **de minimis safe harbor**, as long as you attach a short election statement to your 2026 return and your books treat it as an expense too. For anything bigger, you can deduct the full cost in 2026 through **Section 179** (the 2026 limit is **$2,560,000**) or the **100% special depreciation allowance**. Both of those need the asset **placed in service by December 31, 2026**. Paying for it in December is not enough. It has to be delivered, set up, and ready to use.

This guide is for sole proprietors, single-member LLCs, and small partnerships deciding whether to buy a laptop, camera, tools, or other equipment before the year ends. It covers the federal rules only. We didn't check state income tax rules, which can differ.

# Rule 1: the $2,500 de minimis safe harbor

Normally, the tax law makes you **capitalize** the cost of property you buy for the business and recover it over several years. The IRS's tangible property regulations include a shortcut for small-dollar purchases, the **de minimis safe harbor election**.

What the IRS says about it:

- If you **don't have an applicable financial statement (AFS)**, you can use the safe harbor to deduct amounts up to **$2,500 per invoice or per item** (as substantiated by the invoice). Businesses with an AFS get **$5,000**. An AFS is mainly an audited, CPA-certified statement or one filed with the SEC or another government agency, so most small businesses use the $2,500 figure.
- You must **expense these amounts on your books** under a consistent accounting procedure or policy that existed **at the beginning of the tax year**. Without an AFS, the policy doesn't have to be in writing.
- It doesn't cover **inventory** or **land**.
- The $2,500 isn't a cap on what you can deduct overall. It's a test for each item or invoice.

**How to make the election.** Attach a statement titled **"Section 1.263(a)-1(f) de minimis safe harbor election"** to your **timely filed original return, including extensions**, for the year you paid the amounts. It must include your name, address, and taxpayer identification number, and say you're making the election. The election is made every year, and it isn't an accounting method change, so you don't file Form 3115.

Three details in the regulation itself (Treas. Reg. §1.263(a)-1(f)) catch people:

- **It's all or nothing for the year.** If you elect it, you must apply it to every amount that qualifies that year.
- **You can't revoke it,** and you generally can't make it later on an amended return without IRS consent.
- **Partnerships and S corporations elect at the entity level,** not the partners or shareholders.

# What counts toward the $2,500

The regulation says delivery fees, installation, and similar costs **on the same invoice** as the property are part of its cost for this test. Costs billed on a **separate invoice** don't have to be added. If one invoice covers several items plus a shared delivery charge, you split that charge among the items using a reasonable method, and each item has to pass on its own.

![Two example invoices: a $2,299 laptop with a $150 setup service on the same invoice totals $2,449 and can use the de minimis safe harbor; a $2,350 printer with $275 delivery and installation on the same invoice totals $2,625 and goes over the $2,500 limit](assets/images/year-end-equipment-invoice-test.webp "Example numbers only. Delivery and installation on the same invoice count toward the $2,500 test. Source: Treas. Reg. 1.263(a)-1(f)(3)(i).")

The regulation also has an anti-abuse rule. Splitting one purchase into several invoices just to stay under the limit is the kind of manipulation it targets.

# Rule 2: Section 179 for bigger purchases

Section 179 lets you elect to deduct the cost of qualifying property in the year you place it in service, instead of depreciating it. For tax years beginning in **2026**, IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 and Publication 946 set:

| Section 179 item | 2026 amount |
|---|---|
| Maximum deduction | $2,560,000 |
| Phase-out starts when Section 179 property placed in service exceeds | $4,090,000 |
| Cap for a sport utility vehicle | $32,000 |

For a small business, the dollar limit rarely matters. These rules matter more:

- **More than 50% business use.** If you use the property for both business and personal purposes, Publication 946 says you can elect Section 179 only if business use is **more than 50%** in the year you place it in service. Then you multiply the cost by the business-use percentage.
- **The business income limit.** After the dollar limit, the deduction is limited to your **taxable income from the active conduct of a trade or business** for the year. Any cost you can't deduct because of this limit **carries over** to the next year.
- **Recapture.** If business use drops to 50% or less in a later year of the property's recovery period, you may have to add part of the deduction back to income.
- **How to elect.** Complete **Part I of Form 4562**. For listed property such as most vehicles, complete Part V first.

# Rule 3: the 100% special depreciation allowance

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act reinstated the **100% special depreciation allowance** (often called bonus depreciation) for certain qualified property **acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025**. Publication 946 says that **unless you elect out**, you must take it. Qualified property includes tangible property depreciated under MACRS with a recovery period of **20 years or less** and certain computer software. It can be new or certain used property.

You report it in Part II of Form 4562 (Part V for listed property). If you'd rather spread the deduction over several years, for example because you expect higher income later, you can elect out. Publication 946 says that election is made by class of property and can't be revoked without IRS consent.

# The December trap: placed in service

Section 179 and depreciation follow the year property is **placed in service**, not the year you pay. Publication 946 defines it: property is placed in service when it's **ready and available for a specific use**, even if you haven't used it yet. Its own example is a machine delivered in one year but not installed and operational until the next. It's placed in service in the later year.

![Three hypothetical December purchases: ordered and paid December 15 but delivered January 6 is placed in service in 2027; delivered December 28 and ready to use is placed in service in 2026 even if first used in January; delivered December 28 but not installed until January 10 is placed in service in 2027](assets/images/year-end-equipment-placed-in-service.webp "Hypothetical dates. Section 179 and special depreciation follow the year property is ready and available for use. Source: IRS Publication 946 (2025), Placed in Service.")

So if you're counting on a 2026 deduction for a large purchase:

1. Order early enough that it **arrives in December**.
2. Get it **set up and working** before December 31. For equipment that needs installation, delivery alone may not be enough.
3. Keep the **delivery confirmation** and something that shows the setup date.

The de minimis safe harbor reads differently. The election covers amounts **paid** during the tax year. Even so, buying something you don't need just to get a deduction still costs you the other part of the price.

# Which rule to use

| Your purchase | Rule that usually fits | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| $2,500 or less per item or invoice, expensed on your books | De minimis safe harbor | Election statement attached to the return |
| Over $2,500, business use over 50% | Section 179 or 100% special depreciation | Form 4562, Part I or Part II |
| Over $2,500, but little business income this year | Special depreciation, or Section 179 with carryover | Form 4562 |
| Inventory or land | None of the above | Inventory and land rules apply |

# Year-end checklist

- List every equipment purchase for 2026 with its invoice total, including delivery and installation on the same invoice.
- Mark items of $2,500 or less per item or invoice, and confirm your books expense them.
- Prepare the de minimis election statement to attach to your 2026 return.
- For bigger items, confirm business use is over 50% and that each one is **in service by December 31**.
- Decide with your tax preparer between Section 179 and the special depreciation allowance, and whether to elect out of either.
- Keep invoices, delivery confirmations, and setup records.
