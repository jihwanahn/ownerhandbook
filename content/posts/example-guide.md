Starting an LLC looks like one fee. In practice it is four, and the two that people
forget are the ones that keep charging every year. Here is what the money goes to,
in the order you pay it.

# The four costs, in order

| Cost | Typical range | Recurring? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State filing fee | $50 – $500 | No (one-off) |
| Registered agent | $0 – $150 / year | Yes |
| Annual report | $0 – $800 / year | Yes |
| Business bank account | $0 – $180 / year | Yes |

![Average LLC filing fee by state, 2026](assets/images/llc-filing-fee-breakdown.webp "Filing fees cluster between $90 and $200; the outliers are the states that advertise it.")

The filing fee is the number every comparison article leads with, and it is the least
interesting one — it is paid once.

# What owners actually pay in year one

1. File the formation documents with the secretary of state.
2. Appoint a registered agent (you can usually act as your own).
3. Open a business bank account.
4. Diarise the annual report deadline for the year after you file.

> The annual report is the line item that turns a $150 LLC into a $400 LLC. It recurs
> whether or not the business ever earned anything.

## If you are comparing DIY against a formation service

DIY costs the state fee plus an hour of your time. A formation service typically adds
$100 – $300 of its own fee for filling in the same form. See
[[llc-cost-breakdown|this guide]] for the state-by-state totals, and
[[start-a-business|where to start]] if you have not chosen a state yet.
