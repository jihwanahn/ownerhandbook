Starting an LLC looks like one fee. In practice it is four, and the two that people
forget are the ones that keep charging every year. Here is what the money goes to,
in the order you pay it.

# The four costs, in order

| Cost | Typical range | Recurring? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State filing fee | $35 – $500 | No (one-off) |
| Registered agent | $0 – $250 / year | Yes |
| Annual report or state renewal | $0 – $800 / year | Yes |
| Business bank account | $0 – $360 / year | Yes |

![LLC state filing fees, 2026, six examples](assets/images/llc-filing-fee-breakdown.webp "Most states charge between $50 and $200; Massachusetts is the outlier at $500.")

The filing fee is the number every comparison article leads with, and it is the least
interesting one — it is paid once.

# What owners actually pay in year one

1. File the formation documents with the secretary of state.
2. Appoint a registered agent (you can usually act as your own).
3. Open a business bank account.
4. Put the annual report deadline in your calendar for the year after you file.

> The annual report is the line item that turns a $150 LLC into a $400 LLC. It recurs
> whether or not the business ever earned anything.

## If you are comparing DIY against a formation service

DIY costs the state fee plus an hour of your time. A formation service adds its own
fee on top of the state's — from nothing on a bare-bones tier to around $300 for a
full-service package. If you have not chosen a state yet, start with
[[start-a-business|where to start]].

# Where these numbers come from

Every figure above is the amount charged by the agency that charges it, checked in
October 2026 — not a comparison site's summary of it.

- **Filing fees** are set by each state's secretary of state. The range runs from
  Montana at $35 and Kentucky at $40 to Massachusetts at $500. The six states charted
  above are Massachusetts $500, Texas $300, New York $200, Florida $125, Wyoming $100
  and California $70.
- **Registered agent**: $0 when a member of the LLC acts as its own agent, which most
  states allow. Commercial services run from about $125 to $250 a year.
- **Annual report or state renewal**: no fee in states such as Arizona, Missouri and
  New Mexico; $500 a year in Massachusetts. The $800 at the top of the range is
  California's annual minimum franchise tax, which every LLC organised or doing
  business there pays whether or not it made money.
- **Business bank account**: online-only banks charge no monthly fee. Traditional
  banks charge roughly $15 to $30 a month unless a minimum balance is kept, which is
  where the $360 comes from — most owners pay the $0 end of that range.

None of this covers state income tax, sales tax registration, or the newspaper
publication that New York and a few other states require — those are separate bills
again.
