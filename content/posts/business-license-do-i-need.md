"Do I need a business license?" sounds like one question with a yes or no answer. It
is three questions, and the answer to each is different. A federal licence is almost
never required. A state licence applies to most businesses, in some form. A city or
county licence applies to most businesses with an address customers or inspectors can
visit — the layer owners forget until a letter arrives.

The reason the question is hard online is that it gets answered by state. "It depends
where you live" is true and useless. What decides it is two things you already know:
what you do, and where you do it. This guide separates the three layers, gives you a
four-question check, and puts a number on the cost — including the cost of letting a
licence lapse.

# A licence is not a registration

Three pieces of paper get confused constantly, and they are not the same thing:

- **Formation** — your state accepting an LLC, corporation or partnership. It creates
  the entity. It is not permission to do anything.
- **A tax ID** — the
  [[ein-number-free|EIN]], your federal tax number. It is not a licence either; it is
  needed *to apply for* licences and permits, as the SBA puts it.
- **A licence or permit** — a government's permission to carry out a specific activity
  in its jurisdiction. Federal agencies license regulated activities; states and
  localities license everything else.

That last distinction is where people lose money. Forming an LLC does not register you
with your city, and a city can fine you for operating without its licence even though
your paperwork with the state is perfect. The
[[llc-cost-breakdown|formation and filing fees]] are a different budget line from the
licences.

# The three layers, and who they apply to

| Layer | What it covers | Who issues it | Who needs it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Federal | Business activities a federal agency regulates | USDA, ATF, FAA, FCC, NRC and others | A minority of businesses — see the list below |
| State | General business licence, sales tax, occupational and professional licences | State revenue department or Secretary of State | Most retail, service and professional businesses |
| City / county | Local business licence, zoning, health, sign and use permits | City and county offices | Most businesses with a physical or operating presence |

The SBA's own summary is that "most small businesses need a combination of licenses
and permits from both federal and state agencies," with the local layer sitting on top.

# Layer 1 — the federal licence most businesses never need

At the federal level the rule is narrow. The SBA says a federal licence is required
only when a business activity is one a federal agency regulates. It publishes the
list, and if you are not on it, you are done at this layer:

| Business activity | Issuing agency |
| --- | --- |
| Agriculture — import or interstate transport of animals, animal products, biologics, biotechnology or plants | U.S. Department of Agriculture |
| Alcoholic beverages — manufacture, wholesale, import, or retail sale | Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, plus a local alcohol board |
| Aviation — aircraft operation, air transport, aircraft maintenance | Federal Aviation Administration |
| Firearms, ammunition and explosives — manufacture, sale, import | Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives |
| Fish and wildlife — hunting, trapping, import/export of wildlife products | U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service |
| Commercial fisheries | NOAA Fisheries Service |
| Maritime transportation — transport of people or cargo by sea | Federal Maritime Commission |
| Mining and drilling on federal land | Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement |
| Nuclear energy — production, fuel cycle, materials | Nuclear Regulatory Commission |
| Radio and television broadcasting | Federal Communications Commission |
| Transportation — operation of an oversize or overweight vehicle | U.S. Department of Transportation (directs you to the state office) |

Notice what is missing: an ordinary consultancy, a cleaning business, an online
shop, a landscaping crew, a software company. The federal government does not license
them. If a site has sold you a "federal business license", check it against this table
before you pay.

# Layer 2 — the state licence most businesses do need

Here the SBA is blunt: states "tend to regulate a broader range of activities than the
federal government." Its list of activities commonly regulated at the state or local
level runs through auctions, construction, dry cleaning, farming, plumbing,
restaurants, retail and vending machines — that is, almost every business with
customers.

Two different things sit at this layer, and both count:

- **A general state business licence**, where the state issues one. Washington is a
  clean example, and the next section walks through it.
- **Activity and occupational licences** — a sales tax permit, a contractor licence, a
  cosmetology licence. These are separate from the general licence and are issued by a
  different agency.

Whether your state bundles this into one application or splits it across departments is
why "it depends on your state" is the honest answer. The SBA's advice is to start at
your Secretary of State's website; the practical move is to read your state revenue
department's list of registration triggers, which tells you whether you are in scope at
all.

## What the state layer looks like in one state

Washington's Department of Revenue publishes the triggers, and they read like a
checklist you can test yourself against. You register for a business licence if any of
these is true:

- you need city, county or state endorsements;
- you do business under a name other than your full legal name;
- you plan to hire employees within the next 90 days;
- you sell a product or service that requires collecting sales tax;
- **your gross income is $12,000 a year or more**;
- the state requires you to pay taxes or fees, or you meet its nexus thresholds.

Read the fourth and fifth lines together: that is where most home-based and online
sellers land. A side business under $12,000 a year that sells nothing taxable may sit
below the general-licence line, while a smaller one that sells taxable goods crosses it
on the sales-tax trigger.

There is an order to it. A corporation, partnership, LLC or LLP must file with the
Washington Secretary of State **before** the Business License Application. You leave
with a Unified Business Identifier (UBI), the number used on every later filing. Online
applications take about 10 business days; add two to three weeks with city or state
endorsements; mail can take up to six weeks.

# Layer 3 — the city or county licence almost nobody mentions

This is the layer that gets overlooked, and the SBA flags it directly: if your business
is an LLC, corporation, partnership or nonprofit, you "might need to file for licenses
and permits from the county or city," and local governments are the ones that "determine
registration, licensing, and permitting requirements."

Three things live here that a state licence does not cover:

- **The local business licence itself.** Seattle, for instance, runs its own business
  licence and business and occupation tax on top of the Washington state licence.
- **Zoning and land use.** If you rent, buy or work out of a property, it has to conform
  to local zoning, and the SBA notes that home-based businesses are not exempt — a
  residential address can restrict or forbid certain kinds of work.
- **Physical permits** — a sign permit, a health permit for food, an occupancy or fire
  permit, each issued by a separate city department.

Local rules also move. Seattle, for example, changed how it classifies certain service
activities for its business and occupation tax effective 1 January 2026 — the kind of
change that never appears in a "how to get a business licence" article but changes a
bill.

# The industry overlay

Above the three layers sits one more axis: your industry. The SBA's list of commonly
regulated activities is the short version; here is the practical one.

| If you… | You likely also need | Typically issued by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sell prepared food or drink | A food service or health permit | Local health department |
| Run a daycare or childcare | A childcare licence | State licensing agency |
| Do construction, plumbing or electrical work | A contractor or trade licence | State licensing board |
| Sell tangible goods | A sales tax permit / reseller permit | State revenue department |
| Sell or serve alcohol | A liquor licence | Federal TTB + state alcohol board |
| Operate as a cosmetologist, barber or nail tech | A cosmetology licence | State board |

# Run these four questions against your business

You do not need to read four hundred pages of state code. Answer four questions and the
layers resolve themselves.

1. **What do you actually do?** Check your activity against the federal list above. If
   it is not there, you are done at the federal layer.
2. **Where do you physically operate?** A street address, a home, a vehicle that
   travels to customers, or a laptop — each can require a local licence in a different
   city or county's jurisdiction.
3. **Do you sell taxable goods?** If yes, you need a sales tax permit, and in most
   states that permit is itself a registration that pulls you into the system.
4. **Is your occupation regulated?** Think licence, not company: hairdresser,
   contractor, electrician, nurse, insurance agent, childcare provider.

![The four questions that decide which licences you need](assets/images/business-license-checklist.webp "Answer these four and the three layers stop being abstract. Each yes points at a specific office.")

# What it costs, and what a lapse costs

Licence fees are set by the issuing agency, so they vary by state, city and industry.
Washington's published schedule is a useful benchmark because it shows how small the
numbers usually are — and how the penalty is calculated when you are late:

| Item (Washington state, 2026) | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Open or reopen a business licence | $50 |
| Add an additional location | $0 |
| Add a city non-resident business endorsement | $0 |
| Any other purpose (hiring, trade name, added endorsements) | $10 |
| Annual renewal processing fee | $5 |
| Late renewal penalty | Half the endorsement fee, up to $150 |

Two things follow. First, the licence itself is often the cheapest paperwork you file
this year — the fees that hurt are the activity licences. Second, the cost of forgetting
is calculated: Washington charges half the endorsement fee, capped at $150, for a late
renewal, and the SBA warns that an expired licence is more work to fix than renewing on
time.

Failing to keep a filing current has consequences beyond a late fee. California's
Secretary of State is explicit that failing to file a required Statement of Information
"may result in penalties being assessed by the Franchise Tax Board and suspension or
forfeiture" — the state can suspend the entity, not just bill you.

# Questions owners ask

**Do I need a business licence for an LLC?**
Your LLC formation and your licences are separate. Forming the LLC with the state does
not license you to operate; the licence comes from the same state's revenue department
(if it issues one), and from your city or county. Expect both.

**What if I'm a sole proprietor working under my own name?**
You may sit below the registration line: the SBA notes that if you conduct business as
yourself using your legal name, you may not need to register anywhere. But two things
still apply — a local licence can be required regardless of how the state treats you,
and a name other than your legal name usually triggers registration on its own.

**Is a business licence the same as an EIN?**
No. The EIN is your federal tax number and it is not a licence. The useful point runs the
other way: you generally need the EIN *first*, because both states and banks ask for it
when you apply. Our
[[ein-number-free|EIN guide]] covers getting one for free in minutes.

**Do I need a licence to sell on Etsy, Amazon or Shopify?**
Selling online does not remove the layers — it moves your "location" to your home city
and county. Expect a local business licence and a sales tax permit where you sell into a
state that requires collection, even though a marketplace may collect the tax for you.

**How long does it take?**
It depends on the layers you trigger. Washington publishes about 10 business days for an
online application from its own department, two to three weeks more when city or state
endorsements are involved, and up to six weeks by mail. The longest line is usually the
local one nobody planned for.

# Bottom line

There is no single "business licence". There are three layers — federal, state and
local — and your industry adds a fourth. The federal layer excludes most businesses
entirely; the state layer catches most, usually at a sales-tax or occupation trigger;
the local layer catches nearly everyone with an address. And the cheapest line on the
whole bill is usually the renewal you remember to file on time.

Answer the four questions above, find the one office for each "yes", and put the renewal
date in your calendar the day the licence arrives.
