**Selling handmade goods at a California craft fair generally requires a seller's permit, even for a short event.** If you already have a California permit for a permanent business location, the event normally needs a temporary-location sub-permit instead of a new temporary seller account. The organizer also needs your seller-status information before renting you space.

This guide covers a **one- or two-day fair selling ordinary, nonfood crafts directly to customers**. It does not cover food booths, alcohol, or an ongoing retail business trying to operate through successive temporary permits. The starting point is CDTFA's [seller's permit FAQ](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/faqseller.htm), which applies to individuals as well as LLCs and corporations.

# 1. Choose the right registration route

| Your situation | What to do before the event |
| --- | --- |
| You already hold a California seller's permit for a permanent business location | Register the fair location as a sub-permit under that account. |
| You have no permanent place of business and are selling temporarily | Apply for a temporary seller's permit for the event address and actual selling dates. |
| You operate an ongoing craft business but do not yet have a permit | Ask CDTFA about ongoing registration; a weekend booth does not make the whole business temporary. |

CDTFA's [fairgrounds guidance](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/state-designated-fairgrounds/) gives existing permit holders this route: log in, select the relevant account, choose **More** under **I Want To**, and select **Register a New Location** under **Location Maintenance**.

For a new temporary account, follow the [Temporary Sellers guide](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/temporary-sellers/). Register the event location, enter its start and end dates, and print the location's permit after completing registration. The selling address is the fair's address; the separate mailing address can be your permanent business or residence, as explained on [form CDTFA-410-D](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/cdtfa410d.pdf).

**Planning a longer selling period? Confirm it with CDTFA first.** Its Temporary Sellers webpage describes periods up to 90 days, while the October 2026 edition of [Publication 73, page 6](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub73.pdf) describes the normal period as no longer than 30 days. This guide does not resolve that discrepancy or promise either maximum. Use the dates CDTFA actually approves, and contact the agency before arranging a longer operation.

# 2. Apply through CDTFA and prepare your details

Open [CDTFA Online Services Registration](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/services/registration.htm) and use **Get Started** to begin. The system asks about your business activities to identify the relevant registrations. Answer for the actual business, including existing sales activity, rather than choosing a permit solely because an organizer called it a temporary permit.

Have these details ready, as applicable:

- Identification, SSN or ITIN, and an email address.
- Your products, expected monthly sales, and expected taxable sales.
- Supplier information and the contact who keeps your records.
- Entity details and FEIN when required for your business structure.

CDTFA's [application guidance](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub107/applying-for-a-sellers-permit.htm) also identifies banking information and estimated income. The **seller's permit itself has no application fee**, although CDTFA may require a security deposit. Booth rental and local licensing are separate costs. Same-day issuance may be possible; it is not a processing-time guarantee. Apply before selling, with time to supply the organizer's documents.

# 3. Give the organizer the right paperwork

The [event-operator rules in Publication 111](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub111/) require written seller information before space is rented. Organizers may use CDTFA-410-D or collect the required information another way. Ask which format your fair accepts.

On [CDTFA-410-D](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/cdtfa410d.pdf), complete the event details, your vendor details, the appropriate seller-status box, and the signed certification. Enter your valid permit number when you hold a permit. Give the completed certification to the event operator. **This form documents your status; it does not apply for or replace the permit.**

Do not assume two craft fairs a year are exempt occasional sales. The form's example concerns disposing of unwanted household items no more than twice in twelve months. That is different from making goods or buying stock to sell. If you believe an exemption applies, confirm the basis before selecting the exemption box.

# 4. Prepare for sales and the return

Before configuring your card reader, use CDTFA's [tax-rate lookup by address](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/sales-use-tax-rates.htm). District taxes can increase the rate above the statewide rate. Check the fair's location rather than assuming your home address has the same rate. Keep sales and tax records for the event.

For a **temporary seller's permit**, the [Temporary Sellers guide](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/temporary-sellers/) places the return deadline at the end of the month following the temporary location's closure. For example, a location closing November 15, 2026 would ordinarily have a December 31, 2026 deadline under that rule. Confirm the return and due date shown for your account. This temporary-account example does not change an existing permanent account's assigned filing schedule.

If the venue is a **state-designated fairground**, CDTFA requires separate reporting of the fairground sales amount on the sales and use tax return. That reporting line does not add a tax or fee. Check the official [fairgrounds guide and location list](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/state-designated-fairgrounds/); an event called a craft fair is not, by that name alone, a state-designated fairground.

# 5. Check the local requirements too

A seller's permit is separate from a city or county business license. CDTFA's [FAQ](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/faqseller.htm) directs businesses to the relevant local licensing department. Confirm the event's local requirements with that department and the organizer before paying a nonrefundable booth fee.
