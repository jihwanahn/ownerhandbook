Usually, yes. If your business paid an LLC $2,000 or more for services in 2026, you
send it a Form 1099-NEC unless the LLC is taxed as a corporation. The LLC's Form W-9
tells you which kind it is: line 3a shows how it is taxed, and only a C or S
corporation box takes it out of the 1099 (except for legal fees). For 2026 payments,
the form is due to both the LLC and the IRS by **February 1, 2027**.

The rest of this guide explains how to read the W-9, which exceptions apply, and how
to file before the deadline.

![Does this LLC get a 1099-NEC? A flowchart from W-9 line 3a to the answer](assets/images/llc-1099-decision.webp "Start with line 3a of the LLC's W-9. A disregarded or partnership LLC gets a 1099-NEC at $2,000; an LLC taxed as a corporation usually does not.")

# Why "LLC" alone doesn't answer the question

An LLC is a state legal form, not a federal tax category. For federal tax purposes
an LLC is taxed in one of three ways, and the 1099 rules follow that tax treatment:

- **Single-member LLC with no election.** The IRS disregards it and treats it as its owner.
  The owner is usually an individual, so you report the payment.
- **Multi-member LLC with no election.** It is taxed as a partnership, and you report the payment.
- **LLC that elected corporate treatment (C or S).** The IRS instructions say payments
  to a corporation, "including a limited liability company (LLC) that is treated as a
  C or S corporation," generally aren't reportable.

You don't have to guess which applies. The LLC states it on its W-9.

# Reading line 3 of the W-9

Line 3a of the current Form W-9 (Rev. March 2024) has boxes for individual/sole
proprietor, C corporation, S corporation, partnership, trust/estate, and "LLC," with
a space for the letter C, S, or P. Here is what each answer means for you:

| What the LLC marked on line 3a | How it is taxed | 1099-NEC for $2,000+ in services? |
|---|---|---|
| Individual/sole proprietor (with the LLC's name on line 2) | Disregarded; reported under the owner | Yes |
| LLC, **P** | Partnership | Yes |
| LLC, **C** | C corporation | No, except attorney fees |
| LLC, **S** | S corporation | No, except attorney fees |
| Partnership | Partnership | Yes |

A single-member LLC does **not** check the LLC box. The W-9 instructions say a
disregarded entity "should instead check the appropriate box for the tax
classification of its owner." That's usually "Individual/sole proprietor." The owner's
name goes on line 1 and the LLC's name on line 2. If a one-owner LLC's W-9 says
"LLC" with "C" or "S," the owner has elected corporate tax treatment.

# The $2,000 rule for 2026

For tax years beginning after 2025, the 1099-NEC threshold went up from $600 to
**$2,000**. The IRS says it may be adjusted for inflation starting in 2027. For 2026
payments, file a 1099-NEC for each payee you paid at least $2,000 for services in the
course of your business. Add up everything you paid that payee during the year,
not each invoice separately.

Two details trip people up:

- **The payment must be for your business.** Personal payments, such as hiring a
  painter for your own home, aren't reported.
- **The payment must be for services.** The IRS lists payments for merchandise,
  freight, storage, and similar items as not reportable.

# The exceptions that matter

**Attorneys, even incorporated ones.** The corporate exemption doesn't apply to legal
services. Report attorney fees of $2,000 or more in box 1 of Form 1099-NEC, even if the
law firm is an LLC taxed as a corporation. Gross proceeds paid to an attorney, such as
a settlement you pay through their office, go in box 10 of Form 1099-MISC at $600.

**Medical and health care payments.** These are reportable on Form 1099-MISC, box 6,
even when the payee is a corporation. The threshold is $2,000 for 2026.

**Card and PayPal payments.** If you paid the LLC by credit card, debit card, or a
third-party network such as PayPal, don't put that amount on a 1099-NEC. The IRS
instructions say those payments are reported by the payment settlement entity on
Form 1099-K. Only the amounts you paid by check, ACH, wire, or cash count toward the
$2,000 on your 1099-NEC.

# Get the W-9 before the first payment

Ask for a Form W-9 when you hire the LLC, not in January. The form gives you its
legal name, its tax classification, and its taxpayer identification number (TIN),
which is everything you need to file.

If a payee doesn't give you a TIN, you're required to start **backup withholding**:
withhold 24% of reportable payments and send it to the IRS. If you withheld anything,
you must file a 1099-NEC regardless of the amount, showing the tax in box 4. Having a
signed W-9 on file before the first check avoids this.

# Filling in the 1099 for a single-member LLC

The name and number lines depend on the classification:

- **Disregarded single-member LLC:** the owner's name on the first name line, the
  LLC's name on the second, and the TIN the owner gave on the W-9. That's the owner's
  SSN or EIN, as provided.
- **LLC taxed as a partnership:** the LLC's name and its own EIN.

Copy what the W-9 says. Mismatched name/TIN combinations are a common cause of IRS
notices. If the owner doesn't have an EIN yet, [[ein-number-free|getting an EIN is free and takes minutes online]].

# The deadline: February 1, 2027

The IRS deadline for Form 1099-NEC is January 31, and the same date applies to the copy
you send the payee. If the date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, it moves to the
next business day. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so for 2026 payments both copies are due
**Monday, February 1, 2027**. There's no later e-file date for the 1099-NEC like the
one for most other 1099s.

# How to file: IRIS or paper

**E-filing through IRIS.** The IRS Information Returns Intake System has a free
Taxpayer Portal where you can enter forms by hand or upload a CSV, up to 100 returns
at a time. You need an IRIS Transmitter Control Code (TCC), and the IRS says TCC
applications can take up to 45 days, so apply in the fall rather than in late January.
The IRS is retiring its older FIRE system; starting with the 2027 filing season (tax
year 2026), IRIS is the e-file platform.

**The 10-return rule.** If you file 10 or more information returns in total for the year
(the IRS counts all types of information returns together), you must e-file. Below that, paper is still
allowed.

**Paper.** Copy A must be the official scannable form or a substitute that meets IRS
specifications; non-scannable substitutes can draw a penalty. Send Copy A with a
Form 1096 transmittal, and give the LLC its Copy B.

# Penalties

Penalties apply per return and get larger the later you are. Under the most recent
General Instructions for Certain Information Returns (2025 edition), the amounts
were:

| When the correct return is filed | Penalty per return |
|---|---|
| Within 30 days after the due date | $60 |
| After 30 days, by August 1 | $130 |
| After August 1, or never | $340 |

There are annual caps, which are lower for businesses with average gross receipts of
$5 million or less. A separate penalty applies for not giving the payee its copy.
The IRS can waive penalties for reasonable cause. These amounts are adjusted for
inflation, so check the current General Instructions before relying on them for
returns due in 2027.

# Checklist: does this LLC get a 1099?

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Did you pay it in the course of your business? | Keep going | No 1099 |
| Was it for services (or attorney fees, or medical care)? | Keep going | No 1099-NEC (goods aren't reportable) |
| Did you pay by card or PayPal? | That amount goes on the 1099-K, not on your form | Keep going |
| Is the total by check/ACH/cash $2,000 or more in 2026? | Keep going | No 1099 (unless you backup-withheld) |
| Does W-9 line 3a say C or S corporation? | No 1099, unless it's legal or medical | **Send a 1099-NEC by Feb 1, 2027** |
