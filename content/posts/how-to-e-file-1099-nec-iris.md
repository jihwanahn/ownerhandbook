**Short answer:** you can e-file Form 1099-NEC for free in the IRS's own **IRIS
Taxpayer Portal**, with no software. Before you can log in, you need three things:
an **EIN** for your business, an IRS online account (ID.me or Login.gov) for each person
on the application, and an **IRIS Transmitter Control Code (TCC)**. The TCC is the slow
part. The IRS says a typical application takes up to 45 business days, so apply in
November. For payments you made in 2026, both the IRS filing and the contractor's copy
are due **Monday, February 1, 2027**.

![Four steps to e-file Form 1099-NEC free in IRIS: get an EIN, create an IRS sign-in, apply for an IRIS TCC, then file in the Taxpayer Portal by February 1, 2027](assets/images/iris-1099-nec-four-steps.webp "The path to filing 1099-NECs in IRIS. Step 3, the TCC application, is the one with a waiting period. Source: IRS Publication 5717 (Rev. 9-2026).")

This guide is for a small business filing its first 1099-NECs. If you're still working
out who should get one, start with
[[do-i-need-to-send-1099-to-llc|do I need to send a 1099 to an LLC?]] It covers the
$2,000 threshold for 2026 and the corporation exception.

# Why IRIS, and why this year

For tax year 2026, the IRS says IRIS (the Information Returns Intake System) is the
**only** intake system for information returns. The older FIRE system shuts down at
the end of 2026 and won't accept current-year, prior-year, or correction filings after
that. IRIS has two channels:

- **IRIS Taxpayer Portal:** a free web form. You type each 1099 in, or upload a CSV
  built on the IRS template. This is the one for a small business filing its own forms.
- **IRIS A2A (application to application):** for software and filing services that
  send XML files in bulk. You don't need it unless you're building or running that kind
  of software.

You may also have to e-file. If you file **10 or more information returns** for the
year, e-filing is required, and the IRS counts every type together: four 1099-NECs plus
six W-2s is ten. Below ten, paper is still allowed, but the paper route has its own
catches (see the last section).

# Step 1: Make sure the business has an EIN

The IRIS application asks for the firm's employer identification number. It doesn't
accept a Social Security number or an ITIN in that field, and Publication 1099 says
plainly that "an EIN is required to apply for a TCC." A sole proprietor who has always
used an SSN needs an EIN first. The IRS issues them free online:
[[ein-number-free|what an EIN is and how to get one for free]] walks through it.

The EIN you apply with is the EIN your 1099s will show as the payer. The IRS's
Publication 5717 says an "Issuer" TCC files only for its own business, and the issuer's
EIN must match the EIN on the application.

# Step 2: Create an IRS sign-in for everyone on the application

The IRS uses Login.gov or ID.me to sign in to IRIS. You need only one of them. Creating
the account and verifying your identity takes an SSN (or ITIN for ID.me), a
government photo ID, and a phone or authenticator app. You must be 18 or older.

Every Responsible Official, Authorized Delegate, and contact you plan to list needs
their own account **before** you add them to the application. A one-person business
usually lists one person: the owner, as Responsible Official.

# Step 3: Apply for an IRIS Taxpayer Portal TCC

Go to the IRS page "IRIS application for TCC," sign in, and choose **Individual** on
the *Select Your Organization* page. That's where a new or saved application lives
until it's completed. Then choose *New Application*. You don't have to finish in one
sitting. The application gets a tracking number once you save it.

Have this ready:

| The application asks for | Notes |
|---|---|
| Business structure, legal name, DBA | Use the legal name the IRS has for the EIN |
| EIN | No SSN or ITIN here |
| Business phone and address | Must be a physical location, not a P.O. box |
| Responsible Official's details | SSN or ITIN, date of birth, email, title, phone |
| Role | **Issuer** if you file only for your own business; **Transmitter** if you file for other businesses too |
| Forms | 1099 series (the application groups the forms it covers) |
| Transmission method | Check **Portal** |
| CF/SF | Opt in here if you want the IRS to forward your 1099s to participating states |

To submit, each Responsible Official signs the *Application Submission* page with a
five-digit PIN they create in the system. If a PIN is forgotten, the *Modify PIN* tab
creates a new one.

**How long it takes.** The 2026 Publication 1099 says a typical TCC application is
processed within **45 business days** and that processing times vary. Publication 5717
says to allow up to 45 calendar days, and to call the help desk if the application sits
in *Submitted Pending Review* longer than that. Plan around the longer reading. Counting
45 weekdays and skipping federal holidays, an application filed on November 3 lands
around January 11, one filed on November 16 around January 22, and one filed on
December 1 runs past the February 1 deadline.

![Timeline: an IRIS TCC application filed November 3 finishes around January 11, one filed November 16 around January 22, and one filed December 1 runs past the February 1, 2027 deadline](assets/images/iris-tcc-45-day-window.webp "If the IRS takes the full 45 business days, a TCC application filed after mid-November can run past the February 1, 2027 deadline. Our count: weekdays only, skipping federal holidays.")

**What you get.** A five-character TCC that starts with the letter **D**. You can see
and use it on your Application Summary page before the approval letter arrives by mail.
It works only in the IRIS Taxpayer Portal. A FIRE TCC or an IRIS A2A TCC won't open the
portal. The TCC carries over from year to year, but the IRS deletes it if you don't use
it for three consecutive years, and a deleted TCC can't be reactivated.

# Step 4: Collect what goes on each form

While you wait for the TCC, gather the data. For each contractor you'll need the legal
name, TIN, and address from their Form W-9, and the total you paid them in 2026 by
check, ACH, wire, or cash. Card and PayPal-type payments are reported on Form 1099-K by
the payment processor, not on your 1099-NEC. For 2026 payments the threshold is
**$2,000** per payee for the year.

If a contractor's W-9 shows an LLC, the name lines are easy to get wrong.
[[how-to-fill-out-w9-single-member-llc|How to fill out a W-9 as a single-member LLC]]
shows what a correct one looks like from the contractor's side.

# Step 5: File in the Taxpayer Portal

Sign in from the IRS page "E-file information returns with IRIS." From the dashboard:

1. **Issuer Management.** Save your business as an issuer first. Publication 5717 says
   the payer information has to be entered there before you key in a form. It stays
   saved from year to year.
2. **Start New Form.** Pick *1099-NEC*, then tax year *2026*.
3. **Issuer and Recipient.** Choose your saved issuer, then enter the contractor's
   name, TIN, and address.
4. **Payments.** Enter the amount in box 1a. If you opted into CF/SF, check the CF/SF
   box here and select the state. If you skip it on the form, nothing is forwarded.
5. **State withholdings and payments.** Optional. Fill it in only if you have state
   amounts to report.
6. **Review and Confirm.** The page flags errors. Fix them, then **Submit**. You get a
   receipt ID.

![IRIS Taxpayer Portal flow for one 1099-NEC: Issuer Management, Start New Form, issuer and recipient, payments with the CF/SF box, review and confirm, submit, then check status and download recipient copies](assets/images/iris-portal-1099-nec-flow.webp "Keying one 1099-NEC in the IRIS Taxpayer Portal, from saved issuer to recipient copy. Source: IRS Publication 5717 (Rev. 9-2026), sections 3.3 to 5.")

**Uploading a CSV instead.** Use the *Upload CSV with Form Data* tile, download the IRS
template for 1099-NEC, and read the formatting guidelines linked in the tile's FAQ box.
Only IRS templates are accepted. A file holds one tax year and one form type, up to 250
records. Publication 5717 lists the usual reasons uploads fail:

- Long numbers turning into scientific notation. Format those cells as **Text**.
- Dates without a four-digit year. Use MM/DD/YYYY.
- Periods or # signs in name or address lines, or a line break inside a cell.
- Leading, trailing, or double spaces.
- $0.00 in amount cells. Leave them empty instead.

Save it as *CSV UTF-8 (Comma delimited)*. If you reopen it in Excel, the text formatting
is lost, so make later fixes in a plain text editor.

**The deadline is midnight, not close of business.** Publication 1099 says an IRIS
filing is on time if you submit it by 11:59 p.m. on the due date. Don't wait for that.
If something is rejected on February 1, you have no time to fix it.

# After you submit

Check *View Submitted Forms* for the transmission status:

| Status | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Received by IRS | Still processing | Allow 7 days |
| Accepted | No errors found | Nothing |
| Accepted with Errors | Accepted, but with errors | File corrections |
| Partially Accepted | At least one submission rejected | Replace the rejected part |
| Rejected | Everything rejected | Fix and replace the whole submission |

**Give each contractor their copy.** The portal lets you download recipient copies
as PDFs from the same screen. The IRS doesn't track whether you sent them, so
filing in IRIS doesn't count as furnishing. Downloads come as a ZIP of PDFs, up to 100
forms at a time. Mail or hand each contractor their copy by
February 1, 2027. You don't file a Form 1096 when you use IRIS.

**Fixing a mistake.** For a wrong amount, name, or TIN, select the form and use
*Correct/Replace*. That's a one-step correction. If you used the wrong form type, such as
a 1099-MISC instead of a 1099-NEC, zero out the amounts on a correction and then file the
right form as a new original. Errors in **your own** name or EIN can't be fixed in the
portal. Publication 5717 says to write to the IRS at the Kearneysville, West Virginia
address it lists. Send the contractor a corrected copy too.

**Don't file twice.** If you e-file a return, don't also send it on paper. The IRS
treats that as duplicate filing, and it can lead to penalty notices.

# State filing: what CF/SF does and doesn't do

Through the Combined Federal/State Filing Program, the IRS forwards your 1099-NECs to
participating states at no cost. Two things have to be in place: you opted in on the
TCC application, and you checked CF/SF and the state on each form.

The 2026 participating list in Publication 5717 has 32 jurisdictions. It includes
California, Pennsylvania, Georgia, and the District of Columbia. New York and Illinois
aren't on it. The IRS adds that each state sets and changes its own requirements, and
that checking their criteria and any special data entry rules is the filer's job. Look
at your state revenue department's 1099 page before you rely on CF/SF.

# If you miss the deadline or stick with paper

**No automatic extension.** Most 1099 forms get an automatic 30-day extension with Form
8809. The 1099-NEC doesn't. Publication 5717 says only one *non-automatic* 30-day
extension is available, requested on a **paper** Form 8809. Non-automatic means the IRS
has to approve it, and Form 8809 explains when it can. Late or missing returns carry per-return penalties.
[[do-i-need-to-send-1099-to-llc|The 1099-to-an-LLC guide]] lists the amounts.

**Paper is allowed under 10 returns, but it isn't simpler.** The IRS scans paper Copy A
by machine, so you can't print Copy A of a 1099-NEC from IRS.gov and mail it. You need
the official scannable forms or an approved substitute, plus a Form 1096 transmittal.
That's why IRIS is often easier even for one or two contractors.

# Checklist for a first-time IRIS filer

| When | Do this |
|---|---|
| Now (November) | Confirm you have an EIN; create Login.gov or ID.me accounts for everyone on the application |
| Now (November) | Submit the IRIS application for TCC: Issuer role, Portal method, CF/SF if you want it |
| December | Collect W-9s and add up 2026 payments by check, ACH, wire, or cash for each contractor |
| When the TCC arrives | Sign in, set up Issuer Management, enter or upload the 1099-NECs |
| Mid to late January | Submit, check the status, download and send recipient copies |
| By February 1, 2027 | Both the IRS filing and every contractor's copy are done |
